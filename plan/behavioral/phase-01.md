# Behavioral Phase 1 — Method & Story Mining

**Window:** Oct 2026 (month 1). **Est:** 7.5 h. **Why first:** stories take months to mature: the
learner needs time to recover numbers (dashboards, JIRA, PRs, incident reports, emails), to notice new
story material at work, and to rehearse on a spaced schedule. Mining early also feeds the resume
achievement inventory (CO.1.1) with the same facts.

Row fields: **Goal** · **Prompt/exercise** · **Covers** · **Output** · **Done-when**.

| ID | Type | Session | Est h |
|---|---|---|---:|
| B.1.1 | L | **How behavioral rounds are scored** — Goal: understand what is being measured before producing anything. · Prompt: the learner answers cold, spoken, "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate" (2 min, unscored baseline, saved). Then Claude explains the signals, and the learner self-critiques the baseline against them. · Covers: signals table (README §1); STAR vs CARL and when to use each; headline-first; I vs we; answer lengths (30 s / 2–3 min / deep dive); the probe ladder P1–P6; red flags; the /100 rubric and bands; how Amazon (Bar Raiser), Google (G&L), Atlassian (values round) and Indian HM rounds differ in weight. · Output: `behavioral/mocks/<date>-B.1.1-baseline.md` (transcript + self-critique + Claude's rubric score, kept sealed for comparison at B.7.6). · Done-when: the learner can name the 7 rubric categories and the 6 probe levels unaided and has scored their own baseline | 1.5 |
| B.1.2 | MINE | **Career timeline & project inventory** — Goal: a complete, factual map of the learner's career to mine stories from. · Prompt: build a timeline table (period · team · role · projects · tech · people · artefacts to check). For every project: problem, your part, outcome, a number if any, what went wrong. Homework: dig out artefacts (PRs, design docs, incident notes, performance reviews, appraisal self-assessments, customer emails, certifications). · Covers: all roles incl. internships/college projects if YOE is low; QNu Labs products and domain (quantum-safe: QKD/PQC/key management; learner confirms what's shareable); NDA-safe anonymisation rules. · Output: `behavioral/stories/timeline.md` (learner-written). · Done-when: every role and every project ≥ 1 month long is listed with at least "your part" and "outcome"; artefacts to check are listed | 2 |
| B.1.3 | MINE | **Mining the hard moments: conflict, disagreement, failure** — Goal: find the stories people avoid (and interviewers value most). · Prompt: guided recall per theme, 5 min each, bullet notes only: a peer conflict; a disagreement with your manager (one where you were right, one where you were wrong); a failure/mistake you caused; missed deadline; a code review fight; a decision you regret. For each: what happened, your part, what changed after. Claude probes (P1, P4) but doesn't draft. · Covers: themes 2, 3, 4, 6, 14; how to talk about failure without self-sabotage (bounded, owned, fixed, learned); picking "right-sized" failures (real impact, not catastrophic, not trivial). · Output: raw notes appended to `behavioral/stories/mining-notes.md`. · Done-when: ≥ 2 candidates for each of conflict, manager disagreement, failure | 1.25 |
| B.1.4 | MINE | **Mining ownership moments: ambiguity, leadership, customers, incidents, mentoring** — Goal: find the "raise the bar" stories. · Prompt: guided recall: an ambiguous task with no clear owner or spec; a time you led without authority / influenced another team; delivering under a hard deadline; mentoring or onboarding someone; going beyond the ticket for a customer; a security/quality/production incident (detection → mitigation → root cause → prevention); improving a process/tooling; learning something unfamiliar fast; a calculated risk. · Covers: themes 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13; the security engineer's advantage (incident handling, secure-by-default reviews, crypto migration, compliance deadlines). · Output: appended to `behavioral/stories/mining-notes.md`. · Done-when: ≥ 1 candidate for every theme 1–14 (gaps listed explicitly, with a plan to create material at work, e.g. volunteer to lead a design review) | 1.25 |
| B.1.5 | MINE | **Story-bank selection & coverage matrix** — Goal: choose the 10–14 stories to craft and see the gaps. · Prompt: the learner scores each candidate on: recency (≤ 3 years preferred), personal ownership, measurable impact, level-appropriateness (SDE-2 scope), versatility (themes covered), shareability. Selects 12 (± 2). Builds the matrix: stories × 14 themes; marks primary/backup per theme. Homework list: numbers to recover per story. · Covers: story selection criteria; avoiding over-reliance on one project; one flagship project for the deep dive (B.5); pairing each story with a one-line cue. · Output: `behavioral/stories/README.md` index + coverage matrix filled (status ⬜ for all); `NN-slug.md` stub per chosen story (cue + raw notes only). · Done-when: every theme has a primary and a backup story; the flagship project is chosen; a numbers-to-recover list exists | 1.5 |
